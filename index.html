02 / The possibilities

Eight ways to carry her story.

Some stay close to Grandma’s ring. Others give the same seven stones a completely different rhythm. Start with what feels like your wife; the setting should fit her life, too.

01

Yellow gold · Fan cluster

The Heirloom Echo

Her familiar silhouette, with room for a new life.

The thought behind it

The three-sapphire fan preserves the gesture of her 1950s swirl. Fine prongs keep the marquise outlines open and distinct, while the four diamonds divide into two on each shoulder for balance. A bezel would draw a stronger metal outline; prongs keep this version airy and close to the original. Protective tips belong in the final setting plan.

02

Rose gold · Wishbone

Modern Rose

A softer gold, with thoughtful protection at every point.

The thought behind it

A rose-gold wishbone gives the blue a warm contrast and the fan a lighter, more contemporary stance. The graduated effect comes from the stones’ arrangement, not a requirement to recut them. V-tip prongs are a jeweler’s standard choice for shielding vulnerable marquise points. All four diamonds add light without crowding the wishbone’s open shape.

03

Platinum + yellow gold · Crossover

Bridges Two Eras

Her yellow-gold past meets a platinum future.

The thought behind it

Two crossing metals make the connection tangible: new yellow gold recalls Grandma’s era, while platinum introduces your wife’s chapter. Brushed Florentine-style inner faces add a hand-worked texture beneath the polished edges. Prong settings let all seven stones remain the focus. The crossover needs enough structure for lasting wear, rather than becoming delicate for delicacy’s sake.

04

Yellow gold · Art Nouveau tendrils

The Wave

One flowing line, from her generation to yours.

The thought behind it

Three sapphires travel end-to-end along an Art Nouveau tendril, turning the old swirl into a continuous wave. The four diamonds sit in bezels along the curve—one of the most secure, snag-resistant choices for daily wear when well made. The sapphires still need protected tips. The flowing line makes family continuity feel organic, rather than literal.

05

Yellow gold proposed · East–west

The Modernist

The same seven stones, in a clean everyday rhythm.

The thought behind it

Turning the marquises east–west creates an alternating diamond–sapphire row, beginning and ending with a diamond: exactly four and three. Full bezels surround every stone and protect every marquise point. This is the lowest-profile starting direction for a hands-on life, subject to stone depth. Its modern simplicity lets the inherited blue speak without a raised cluster.

06

Yellow gold proposed · Vertical trilogy

The Trilogy

Past, present, future—held in one familiar form.

The thought behind it

A classic vertical trilogy gives each sapphire its own place along the finger, with the four diamonds in supporting roles. Past, present and future is a traditional three-stone reading, especially meaningful for an heirloom. Prongs preserve the distinct marquise shapes; tip protection and careful spacing keep the elegant line practical. Nothing needs to compete with the symbolism.

07

Two-tone gold proposed · Twisted vine

The Vine

Two families intertwine; the old stones keep growing.

The thought behind it

The marquises naturally become three leaves, with four diamond dewdrops tucked into a twisted, two-tone vine. Its intertwining strands stand for two families joining, not one history replacing another. Small prongs keep the leaf shapes legible. The jeweler should shelter their points and smooth the vine’s junctions, so the nature-inspired movement is comfortable as well as meaningful.

08

White gold · Milgrain fan

The Lotus

A little more presence, without a single extra stone.

The thought behind it

The three-sapphire fan opens like a lotus in white gold. Four diamonds form an open, halo-like accent—not a full diamond halo—while the surrounding metal gives the ring more presence. Milgrain’s tiny metal beads echo the hand-finished detail of mid-century pieces; they are not extra stones. Fine prongs keep the blue distinct from its softly textured frame.

    04 / From a thought to her hand

    What happens next

    The pictures open the conversation. A good jeweler makes the design truly belong to her.

    1. 1

      Pick a direction.

      Choose one favorite, or two that share something she loves. Talk about her usual jewelry, her daily routine and whether this ring needs to sit beside a wedding band. Borrow details thoughtfully; the seven original stones stay the constant.

    2. 2

      Let the real stones lead the CAD.

      The jeweler inspects the heirloom, plans careful removal, and measures every stone in person—including depth and any differences between the marquises. Those actual dimensions shape the CAD. Review ring size, band clearance, point protection and height before approving the final seven-stone layout.

    3. 3

      Cast, set, and begin again.

      After approval, the jeweler casts the new mounting in new metal, completes any assembly and finishing, and sets her three sapphires and four diamonds. A final fit and security check comes before the handover—then it’s your wife’s turn to make memories with it.
